Citizen Services Portal

Sample case study

A public institution wanted residents to complete common requests online, in the language they prefer, and to see where each request stands. We designed and built the portal, connected it to the systems already in use, and handed it over with the documentation to run it.

This is a sample study that shows how we present our work. The client, figures, and details are illustrative and will be replaced by a published engagement.

01 Client

A public institution with services people depend on.

The institution handles permits, certificates, and registrations for residents and businesses. Most requests started at a counter, on paper, during office hours.

Its teams knew the process well. What they lacked was one place where a resident could start a request, pay for it, and follow it to a decision.

02 Challenge

Many forms, many counters, one resident.

Each service had its own form, its own queue, and its own way of telling people what happened next. Residents repeated the same details at every step.

The portal had to work on low-cost phones, in Kinyarwanda, English, and French, and for people who use assistive technology. It also had to sit in front of existing records and payment systems without replacing them.

03 Approach

Start from the task, not the org chart.

We mapped the requests residents make most often and designed each one as a short, guided task. Staff reviewed every screen against how the work is actually done.

Integration was built and tested in a controlled environment first. Each release was demonstrated on real scenarios before it reached the public.

04 What we built

Four parts, one service.

  1. 01

    Resident portal

    Guided applications with saved progress, document upload, and a status page for every request.

  2. 02

    Staff workspace

    One queue for incoming requests, with assignment, notes, and decision records.

  3. 03

    Payments and notices

    Payment through the existing gateway, with SMS and email updates at each stage.

  4. 04

    Content management

    Service pages that staff can update in three languages without a developer.

05 Technology

What it runs on.

  • TypeScript
  • React
  • Node.js
  • PostgreSQL
  • REST APIs
  • Docker
  • SMS gateway

06 Outcome

A service residents can finish online.

  • Residents start, pay for, and track a request from one account, in the language they choose.
  • Staff work from a single queue instead of separate inboxes and paper files.
  • Every decision is recorded with who made it and when.
  • The institution holds the source code, architecture notes, and runbooks.
