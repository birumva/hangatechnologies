This is a sample study that shows how we present our work. The client, figures, and details are illustrative and will be replaced by a published engagement.

01 Client

A programme run by many partners.

The programme works through implementing partners across several districts. Each partner collects data on activities, participants, and spending.

Programme managers report to funders every quarter and need figures they can stand behind.

02 Challenge

Five spreadsheets, five versions of the truth.

Each partner kept its own files in its own format. Consolidating them took weeks and the totals rarely matched.

Field teams often work without a connection, and some of the data identifies individuals. Access had to be limited by role and by partner.

03 Approach

Agree the definitions, then build the pipeline.

We began with the indicators: what each one means, who owns it, and where the number comes from. That became the data model.

Delivery ran in phases. The first connected two sources and one report. Each later phase added sources once the previous one was trusted.

04 What we built

From field entry to funder report.

  1. 01

    Field capture

    A mobile form that works offline and syncs when a connection returns.

  2. 02

    Data pipeline

    Scheduled imports from partner files and systems, with validation and error reports.

  3. 03

    Dashboards

    Views for field, programme, and management teams, limited to what each may see.

  4. 04

    Report exports

    Quarterly tables produced in the funder's format, with the source of each figure.

05 Technology

What it runs on.

  • Python
  • PostgreSQL
  • dbt
  • React
  • Offline-first mobile
  • Scheduled pipelines

06 Outcome

One set of figures, owned by the programme.

  • Managers see current field data without waiting for a manual consolidation.
  • Each figure in a report can be traced back to its source.
  • Partners see their own data and nothing they should not.
  • The programme team was trained to add indicators and reports themselves.
