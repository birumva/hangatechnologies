Document Intake Automation

Sample case study

A regulated organisation received thousands of application documents a year and keyed each one by hand. We built a supervised intake workflow that reads and sorts the documents, then puts every record in front of an officer before anything is saved.

This is a sample study that shows how we present our work. The client, figures, and details are illustrative and will be replaced by a published engagement.

01 Client

A regulator where every record counts.

The organisation licenses and supervises firms in its sector. Applications arrive as scanned forms, certificates, and supporting letters.

Officers are accountable for each decision. Any automation had to support that accountability, not blur it.

02 Challenge

Skilled officers, hours of retyping.

Officers spent much of their day copying details from documents into the records system. Backlogs grew at peak periods and small errors were hard to trace.

The data is sensitive. It could not leave the organisation's control, and every automated step needed an explanation an auditor could follow.

03 Approach

Automate the reading, keep the decision.

A short discovery engagement set the scope: which document types, which fields, and what a correct result looks like. We agreed the evaluation criteria before building.

The workflow proposes a classification and the extracted fields. An officer confirms or corrects each one. Low-confidence items are flagged, never guessed.

04 What we built

A workflow with a person at every gate.

  1. 01

    Classification

    Incoming files sorted by document type, with a confidence score an officer can see.

  2. 02

    Field extraction

    Names, dates, and reference numbers read from each document and shown beside the original.

  3. 03

    Review queue

    A side-by-side screen where officers accept, correct, or reject each record.

  4. 04

    Audit trail

    A log of what the system proposed, what the officer decided, and why.

05 Technology

What it runs on.

  • Python
  • Document OCR
  • Language models
  • PostgreSQL
  • Role-based access
  • Private hosting

06 Outcome

Less retyping, the same accountability.

  • Officers review prepared records instead of typing them from scratch.
  • Every saved record has a named reviewer and a traceable history.
  • The existing records system was integrated, not replaced.
  • Evaluation results and the test set were handed over with the system.
