Paradis Ahoi: AI Booking Proof of Concept

Case study

Paradis Ahoi opened in Leipzig-Reudnitz in November 2025, at Dresdner Straße 49. We built a three-month proof of concept for an AI-enabled booking and reservation system: locally hosted language models power agentic workflows that answer guest questions and take booking requests, while staff keep the final say on service decisions.

01 Client

A new bar in Leipzig’s east.

Paradis Ahoi is a bar at Dresdner Straße 49 in Reudnitz, in a space that previously housed the Fischladen bar and later the Gastgeber concept restaurant. It opened to guests on 14 November 2025.

It is open from late afternoon into the night through the week, which means guests ask about tables and opening times at hours when staff are busy serving.

02 Challenge

Questions arrive during service.

A new venue gets the same questions again and again: are you open, can we book a table, can we come as a group. Answering them takes staff away from guests in the room.

Booking decisions depend on the evening: how full the bar is, the size of the group, and what the team can handle. An automated system must not promise what staff have not agreed.

Guest messages contain personal details, so the owners wanted the language models to run locally rather than send conversations to an external AI service.

03 Approach

Agents that ask, staff who decide.

We designed the workflow as a set of agentic steps: understand the guest’s request, collect the details a booking needs, check them back with the guest, and pass the request to the team.

General questions are answered from approved venue information. Anything that commits the venue, such as accepting a booking or a large group, goes to a member of staff for a decision.

As a proof of concept, the aim was to show that the workflow works on locally hosted models and fits how the bar runs, before any decision on a full rollout.

04 Local models

Guest data stays on site.

The language models are hosted on premises. Conversations used for inquiries and booking requests are not sent to an external AI provider for processing.

That choice shaped the architecture: the agentic workflow had to run reliably on local infrastructure while still giving staff a clear review step before any confirmation reaches the guest.

05 What we built

A booking flow with a human in the loop.

  1. 01

    Conversational inquiries

    Guests ask about opening hours, the venue, and visiting in natural language and get answers from approved information.

  2. 02

    Booking requests

    The agent collects date, time, group size, and name, confirms them with the guest, and records a request.

  3. 03

    Staff review

    Each request is shown to staff, who accept, adjust, or decline it. Only then is the guest told the outcome.

  4. 04

    Local model hosting

    The language models run on locally hosted infrastructure, so guest conversations are not sent to an external AI provider.

06 Technology

What it runs on.

  • Locally hosted LLMs
  • Agentic workflows
  • Conversational booking
  • Human review of service decisions

07 Outcome

A tested path to AI-assisted bookings.

  • The proof of concept shows guest questions and booking requests handled by agentic workflows on local models.
  • Staff keep control of every decision that commits the venue.
  • Guest conversations stay on locally hosted infrastructure.
